Graph-theoretic structure of the Lean results

At the level of declarations, the proof constitutes a directed graph on 128,479 vertices and 573,122 edges; we note here that an edge from $u$ to $v$ implies that the statement or proof of $u$ refers to $v$. The density of this graph is $3.5 \times 10^{-5}$, and its in-degree distribution is heavily tailed, in the sense that 58% of declarations are cited at most once, about 0.4% are cited at least a hundred times, and the most cited is used 6,641 times. Since Lean forbids circular dependencies, the graph is acyclic apart from 27 small cycles introduced by the name matching that we used to construct it; the shortest paths from riemannHypothesis reach depth 56, while the longest dependency chain has 139 steps.

Contracting each module group to a vertex gives a connected graph on 771 vertices and 3,862 edges, with mean degree 10, diameter 9, mean distance 3.1 and clustering coefficient 0.52. This gives a small-world pattern of a few hubs joining many dense local clusters. Then, the degree assortativity of the graph, $$r \;=\; \frac{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{e} j_e k_e \;-\; \Big[\frac{1}{M}\sum_{e} \tfrac{1}{2}(j_e + k_e)\Big]^2}{\frac{1}{M}\sum_{e} \tfrac{1}{2}\big(j_e^2 + k_e^2\big) \;-\; \Big[\frac{1}{M}\sum_{e} \tfrac{1}{2}(j_e + k_e)\Big]^2},$$ where the sums run over the $M$ edges and $j_e, k_e$ are the degrees of the two endpoints of the edge $e$, is $r = -0.13$, so such hubs attach primarily to small groups. Five groups hold 65% of the declarations, and Development.AbelianVarietyTheory.Proofs alone has normalised betweenness 0.48.

Pillars of argumentation

We call a pillar one of the six results in the upper layers of the tree on which the remainder of the proof primarily relies. These include the analytic reduction (rh_of_every, critLine), the Galois representations of Row0b, the theta lifts of TLiftK, the local Langlands correspondence (recTExists), the Selmer bound VWin, and the bridge from VWin to nonvanishing. A declaration is reached by $p$ if it can be arrived at from $p$ by following references, that is, if it appears in the statement or proof of $p$, or in the statement or proof of a declaration that does so, and so on down to the foundations. For a pillar $p$ let $D(p)$ be the set of declarations reached by $p$, and let $$E(p) \;=\; D(p) \setminus \bigcup_{q \neq p} D(q)$$ be its exclusive support. That is, $E(p)$ is the component of the formalization that serves $p$ and is exclusive to $p$. The six pillars that we identified together reach 128,464 of the 128,479 declarations under riemannHypothesis.

Three findings emerge from this pillar-based characterization. First, the pillars of local Langlands and of the theta lifts are not independent blocks: $D(\texttt{recTExists})$ lies entirely inside the support of the Galois representations, as do 289 of the 291 declarations under TLiftK, so both enter the proof as ingredients of the construction of Galois representations, which alone holds 78,765 exclusive declarations, or 61% of the proof. Second, the Selmer bound VWin reaches 28,257 declarations, of which 14,315 are shared with the Galois block and 13,934 are its own, concentrated in archimedean base change, block decompositions and inertia eigenvalues. Third, the bridge from VWin to nonvanishing is almost disjoint from the rest of the proof: 18,825 of its 19,182 declarations (98%) are exclusive, and they lie in arithmetic geometry, namely embedded resolution of singularities, the arithmetic Hilbert–Samuel theorem and ampleness of heights. If the proof contains a genuinely new argument, these two blocks, the Selmer bound and the height-theoretic bridge, are where this new argument might lie.

Statements and the analytic reduction

The statements

The development closes with five theorems in Main.lean, each stated against definitions in Statements.lean that import only Mathlib. The first is Mathlib's own RiemannHypothesis,

$$\forall s \in \mathbb{C},\quad \zeta(s) = 0,\;\; s \notin \{-2(n+1) : n \in \mathbb{N}\},\;\; s \neq 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac12 ,$$

and the remaining four concern Dirichlet $L$-functions $L(s,\chi) = $ DirichletCharacter.LFunction $\chi\, s$, the meromorphic continuation in Mathlib. DirichletLNonvanishingRightOfHalf asserts $L(s,\chi) \neq 0$ for every $N \geq 1$, every $\chi \bmod N$ and every $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12$, excepting $(\chi, s) = (1, 1)$; DirichletLZerosOnLine asserts that zeros with $0 < \operatorname{Re} s < 1$ lie on $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac12$, and PrimitiveDirichletLZerosOnLine restricts this to primitive $\chi$. Every one of these is a consequence of the single proposition

$$\mathrm{EveryCharacter}: \qquad \forall N \geq 1,\ \forall \chi \bmod N,\ \forall s,\quad \operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12,\ (\chi \neq 1 \text{ or } s \neq 1) \;\Longrightarrow\; L(s,\chi) \neq 0 ,$$

(UpperTheory.EveryCharacter), which is proved as UpperTheory.FinalTheorems.everyCharacter. The whole of the depth of the development lies beneath this proposition; the passage from it to the five statements is short and uses only Mathlib.

The analytic reduction

Formalism.VW.rh_of_every specialises EveryCharacter to the trivial character modulo $1$, for which $L(s,1) = \zeta(s)$ by LFunction_modOne_eq, and so obtains $\zeta(s) \neq 0$ on $\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12$, $s \neq 1$. Formalism.VW.rh_of_right then reflects this through Mathlib's functional equation riemannZeta_one_sub, applied at $1 - s$:

$$\zeta(s) \;=\; 2\,(2\pi)^{-(1-s)}\,\Gamma(1-s)\,\cos\!\Big(\frac{\pi(1-s)}{2}\Big)\,\zeta(1-s).$$

For a zero $s$ with $\operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac12$ each factor on the right is nonzero: $\Gamma(1-s) \neq 0$ because $\operatorname{Re}(1-s) > 0$; $\zeta(1-s) \neq 0$ by hypothesis, since $\operatorname{Re}(1-s) > \tfrac12$ and $1 - s \neq 1$; and the cosine vanishes only at $s = -2k$ with $k \in \mathbb{Z}$, where $k \leq -1$ contradicts $\operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac12$, $k = 0$ is excluded by $\zeta(0) = -\tfrac12$, and $k \geq 1$ is a trivial zero, excluded by hypothesis. The case $\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12$ is the hypothesis itself, so every nontrivial zero has $\operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac12$.

The critical-line statement for primitive characters, Formalism.VW.critLine, runs the same argument through the completed $L$-function $\Lambda(s,\chi) = \gamma(s,\chi)\,L(s,\chi)$, with $\gamma(s,\chi) = \Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+a)$ and $a \in \{0,1\}$ the parity of $\chi$. By Formalism.VW.gammaFactor_ne_zero, $\gamma(s,\chi) \neq 0$ for $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$, so a zero of $L(s,\chi)$ in $0 < \operatorname{Re} s < \tfrac12$ is a zero of $\Lambda(s,\chi)$; Mathlib's functional equation for primitive characters, IsPrimitive.completedLFunction_one_sub, gives $\Lambda(1-s,\chi^{-1}) = \varepsilon(\chi)\,\Lambda(s,\chi)$, so $\Lambda(1-s,\chi^{-1}) = 0$ and hence $L(1-s,\chi^{-1}) = 0$ with $\operatorname{Re}(1-s) > \tfrac12$, contradicting EveryCharacter for the primitive character $\chi^{-1}$. Imprimitive characters are reduced to primitive ones in UpperTheory.Top.imprimitive through Mathlib's LFunction_changeLevel,

$$L(s,\chi) \;=\; L(s,\chi^{*}) \prod_{p \mid N} \big(1 - \chi^{*}(p)\,p^{-s}\big),$$

where $\chi^{*}$ is the primitive character inducing $\chi$; each factor is nonzero for $\operatorname{Re} s > 0$ because $|\chi^{*}(p)\,p^{-s}| = p^{-\operatorname{Re} s} < 1$.

The reduction is therefore entirely classical and entirely carried by Mathlib: the functional equations, the Gamma function and the identification $L(s,1) = \zeta(s)$ are library results. A reader assessing the development can take this layer as settled and direct attention to EveryCharacter.

The logical skeleton and its quantifier structure

The logical skeleton as a derivation

Lean's logic is dependent type theory, in which a theorem is a closed term whose type is the proposition it proves and an implication $A \to B$ is proved by a function from proofs of $A$ to proofs of $B$. Above the deep layers, however, the development uses only a fragment that reads naturally as first-order natural deduction: implication elimination, conjunction and structure introduction, universal instantiation, and definitional unfolding. The top of the proof can therefore be written out as an explicit derivation whose every line is a Lean declaration.

Quantifier structure

The statements differ sharply in logical complexity. The Riemann Hypothesis and EveryCharacter are universal statements with a quantifier-free matrix over $\mathbb{C}$:

$$H \equiv \forall s \in \mathbb{C}\; \big(\zeta(s) = 0 \wedge \neg\exists n\,(s = -2(n+1)) \wedge s \neq 1 \to \operatorname{Re} s = \tfrac12\big),$$
$$C \equiv \forall N \geq 1\; \forall \chi \bmod N\; \forall s \in \mathbb{C}\; \big(\operatorname{Re} s > \tfrac12 \wedge (\chi \neq 1 \vee s \neq 1) \to L(s,\chi) \neq 0\big).$$

The Selmer bound $V$ has a much deeper prefix. Writing $\mathrm{WP}$ for WinPair, $\mathrm{CF}$ for IsChiField and $\mathrm{VV}$ for VVle, its definition (UpperTheory.Top.VWin, UpperTheory.Top.VWinAt) is, after moving quantifiers outward,

$$\begin{aligned} V \;\equiv\; \forall \psi\; \forall \varepsilon\; \forall e\; \Big[\, &\Phi(\varepsilon, e) \;\to\; \exists J\; \exists X_0\; \forall X\; \forall q\; \forall d\; \Big( X \geq X_0 \wedge \mathrm{WP}(\varepsilon, X^{1/J}, q, d) \;\to \\ &\exists Y\; \big( X^{1/J} \leq Y \wedge Y^2 \leq X \;\wedge\; \forall x\; \forall E\; ( Y \leq x \leq Y^2 \wedge \mathrm{CF}(\psi, e(x), q, d, E) \\ &\qquad\qquad \to\; \mathrm{VV}(E, \lfloor x^{401} \rfloor, \sqrt{\exp(x^{\varepsilon})}) ) \big) \Big) \Big], \end{aligned}$$

where $\Phi(\varepsilon, e)$ is $0 < \varepsilon < \tfrac{1}{52} \wedge \forall x \geq 1\; x^{2+8\varepsilon} \leq 2^{e(x)} \leq 2x^{2+8\varepsilon}$. The prefix alternates five times, $\forall\exists\forall\exists\forall$, and it is not first order in the strict sense: $e$ ranges over functions $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{N}$, $E$ ranges over number fields, which in Lean are types, and $\mathrm{VV}$ itself quantifies over all choices of local Galois-cohomological data $\mathrm{LData}$ and over all finite sets of primes. The first-order reading is accurate for the derivation above, which manipulates these statements only as wholes, and it becomes an approximation as soon as one looks inside them.

The asymmetry is the shape of the argument. The deep arithmetic of the development produces a statement of high quantifier complexity, uniform in the character, the scale and the auxiliary primes, and the nonvanishing bridge converts it into a statement of the simplest universal form. Assessing the development therefore comes down to two questions of different kinds: whether the arithmetic genuinely establishes $V$ with the stated uniformity, and whether the bridge from $V$ to $C$ uses that uniformity correctly.

Metatheory

The logic, its axioms and its strength

The derivation above is first order only at the surface. The proof lives in Lean 4's kernel logic, a dependent type theory in the family of the calculus of inductive constructions: an impredicative and proof-irrelevant universe $\mathrm{Prop}$ of propositions, a non-cumulative hierarchy of universes $\mathrm{Sort}\,u$, inductive families, built-in quotient types, and definitional equality decided by $\beta$, $\delta$, $\eta$, $\iota$ and $\zeta$ reduction. Above this sit exactly three axioms in standard use, propositional extensionality propext, the soundness of quotients Quot.sound (from which function extensionality is derived), and global choice Classical.choice. The archive declares no further axiom: a search of all 175,187 files finds no axiom declaration, and the files used by the proof contain no native_decide, which would otherwise add the axiom Lean.ofReduceBool and with it trust in compiled code. After a build, #print axioms riemannHypothesis would list the axioms actually used; on the evidence of the source it can list at most these three.

Strength. Lean's type theory with $n + 1$ universes has a set-theoretic model in ZFC together with $n$ strongly inaccessible cardinals, the universes being interpreted by the corresponding Grothendieck universes (Carneiro, The Type Theory of Lean, 2019). The proof reaches $\mathrm{Type}\,2$ explicitly, for instance in the category of étale sheaves of modules GlobalTheory.EtC.Shv, so as written it is a theorem of a theory of strength at most ZFC plus finitely many inaccessible cardinals. Uses of universes of this kind, which come from category theory, have in comparable cases been eliminated in favour of weak fragments of set theory, but no such elimination has been carried out for this development (interpretation).

Classical content. The proof is thoroughly classical. Choice and excluded middle are used throughout, even at the top: Formalism.VW.critLine argues by contradiction on $\operatorname{Re} s \neq \tfrac12$, and some 15,000 definitions are marked noncomputable. The development therefore carries no algorithmic content that could be extracted, and its meaning rests on classical semantics.

Arithmetical complexity. This last point matters less than it might, because of the logical form of the conclusion. The Lean statement is Mathlib's analytic formulation, but by a theorem of classical analysis the Riemann Hypothesis is equivalent to a $\Pi^0_1$ sentence of arithmetic; one such form is Lagarias's criterion

$$\forall n \geq 1:\qquad \sigma(n) \;\leq\; H_n + e^{H_n} \log H_n ,$$

with $\sigma$ the sum of divisors and $H_n$ the $n$-th harmonic number. A $\Pi^0_1$ sentence is refuted, if false, by a single finite computation. It is absolute between models of set theory with the same natural numbers, so neither choice nor large universes can make a false one provable in a theory that proves only true $\Sigma^0_1$ sentences, and ZFC with finitely many inaccessibles is standardly taken to have that property. The equivalence with the analytic form is a metatheorem about the statement and is not formalised in the development (interpretation).

What a kernel check certifies. If the development builds, the kernel has checked every declaration from the axioms above, so the formal statement holds relative to three things: the correctness of the kernel; the faithfulness of the statement, which here rests only on Mathlib's definitions of $\zeta$ and of Dirichlet $L$-functions, since Statements.lean imports nothing else; and the absence of environment manipulation. On the last point the archive contains five BuildAid files that use run_cmd and modifyEnv to add the project's namespaces to the deny list of the library-suggestion tactic, which affects only automated search, along with one use of addDecl, three custom elab commands and nineteen macros. Elaboration-time code of this kind is precisely what an independent replay of the compiled environment through a separate checker such as lean4checker or Lean4Lean excludes, since such a replay re-checks every declaration in the kernel without running any of it.

Names and content. The kernel certifies derivations from what the definitions actually say, so a name that overstates its content cannot make the proof unsound; it changes only how the formal proof relates to the informal narrative. Several such gaps are visible in the source. GlobalTheory.Published.UnusedSlot72 is defined as True, and some hypothesis bundles declare their field siegelEisensteinSimplePole with that type, so the field carries no content. The local Langlands notion recT constrains only representations satisfying Good, with UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.junkEx supplying placeholder parameters elsewhere. And UpperTheory.LiteratureTheorems.OpenImageCondition has a single field, while the sixty lemmas *_of_open that take it as an argument ignore it and are discharged by separately proved theorems *_holds. In each case, the formal theorem depends on internally proved statements whose exact content, which may be narrower than the named literature result, is what a referee must compare with the published mathematics.

The nonvanishing bridge

1. The target statement and the top-level assembly

The target is

$$\texttt{EveryCharacter} :\iff \forall N\ge 1,\ \forall \chi \bmod N,\ \forall s,\ \ \operatorname{Re}s>\tfrac12,\ (\chi\neq 1 \lor s\neq 1)\ \Longrightarrow\ L(s,\chi)\neq 0,$$

3. The analytic mechanism

The test function. For a level $\Lambda$ (Formalism.HC.Level.Gm),

$$\mathrm{Gm}_\Lambda(y)=G_\zeta(y)+\sum_{\psi'\in\Lambda\setminus\{1\}} w(\psi')\,\operatorname{Re} g_{\psi'}(y),\qquad w(\psi')=\begin{cases}1&\psi'=\psi_K\\ (3o+1)^{-1}&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}$$

with $o=\Lambda.o$ (for Level.cyc this is the order of $\langle\psi\rangle$, see Level.ofSub). By Formalism.Gz,

$$G_\zeta(y)=\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)\Big(\frac yn-1\Big)-y\log y+(1+\gamma)y,$$

and by Formalism.HC.An.gChi, for $\chi\neq 1$,

$$g_\chi(y)=\sum_{n\le y}\Lambda(n)\chi(n)\Big(\frac yn-1\Big)+y\,\frac{L'}{L}(1,\chi).$$

These are Cesàro-weighted (Riesz-mean) Chebyshev functions with their main terms removed. Their Mellin transforms are $-\frac{1}{s(s-1)}\frac{L'}{L}(s)$ up to polar terms (Landau.mellin_Gchi').

Landau's principle. Formalism.HC.Landau.zero_free_of_Chebyshev_growth proves Level.ChebyshevGrowthImpliesZeroFree: if $\mathrm{Gm}_\Lambda(y)\le y^\theta$ for all large $y$, then $\zeta$ and every $L(s,\psi')$ with $\psi'\in\Lambda$ are zero-free on $\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$. The proof passes through landau_family, which applies landau_entire to $P=\zeta_1^{R}\prod_{\xi}L(s,\xi)$. The Dirichlet coefficients of $-P'/P$ are $\Lambda(n)\big(R+\sum_\xi \xi(n)\big)$, and these are nonnegative because $\Lambda$ is closed under inversion and the weights are chosen to match (hki, hkw). This is the classical Landau theorem on Dirichlet integrals with nonnegative coefficients.

Windowed form. The bridge needs the bound only on windows. Formalism.HC.Level.FormBAt λ states: if $\{x:\mathrm{Gm}_\Lambda(x)\le x^\theta\}$ contains intervals $[Y,Y^{\lambda}]$ for arbitrarily large $Y$ (HasWindows), then $\Lambda$ is zero-free above $\theta$. UpperTheory.Comparison₂.formB proves this for every $\lambda>1$ through Deduction.zeroFree_of_rpow_window and Deduction.lemmaL. Underneath sits Deduction.landau_window. With $u=\log y$, that argument bounds high-order Taylor coefficients $\Phi^{(k)}(\sigma)/k!$ of the Laplace transform of $g(u)=\mathrm{Gm}(e^u)$. The weight $u^k e^{-\sigma u}$ concentrates on $[U,(1+\delta)U]$ (landau_window_key, landau_tails), and the resulting coefficient growth is set against the pole of $\Phi$ at the rightmost zero (landau_window_contra, landau_dichotomy, landau_nearest). (interpretation) This is a Pólya–Landau "Taylor coefficient" argument. It replaces the global one-sided bound of Landau's theorem by a bound on a sparse set of long logarithmic windows.

What is contradicted. Suppose $L(\rho,\psi)=0$ with $\operatorname{Re}\rho>1/2$. The contradiction is with the inequality

which is ChebyshevInequalityOnWindows (TopWin.lean). Through FormB, inequality $(\ast)$ forces zero-freeness above $1/2+(e_D+e_L+7)\varepsilon<\operatorname{Re}\rho$.

The arithmetic side: the Selmer bound and the Galois representations

Scope and conventions

This note describes the arithmetic half of the development: the deduction of the Tate–Shafarevich length bound UpperTheory.Top.VWin from the Galois-representation statement UpperTheory.GalStar.Row0b, and the proof of Row0b itself. Every claim is tied to a Lean declaration. Sentences marked "(interpretation)" are readings of the formal statements and are not asserted by the code. The top-level use is in UpperTheory.FinalTheorems.everyCharacter_of_row, which feeds both Row0b and UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.recTExists into the analytic half, together with UpperTheory.One.vwin_of_row h : VWin. The target statement VWin

For a number field $E$, a prime $l$ and $e\in\mathbb Z$, an Formalism.GaloisCohomology.LData $D$ consists of the set $S$ of places of $E$ above $l$, chosen decomposition data at those places, and a character $\chi:G_{E,S}\to\mathbb Z_l^\times$ restricting to the cyclotomic character. Then Formalism.GaloisCohomology.Sha is

$$\text{Ш}^1(D,e)=\ker\Big(H^1\big(G_{E,S},(\mathbb Q_l/\mathbb Z_l)(\chi^{-e})\big)\to\prod_{v\mid l}H^1(G_v,\cdot)\Big),$$

and shaLen is its $\mathbb Z_l$-length in $\mathbb N\cup\{\infty\}$ (via Formalism.GaloisCohomology.Sha1, which intersects kernels of restriction maps). Note that $S$ consists only of places above $l$ (field hS).

UpperTheory.Top.VVle E t B says that for every algebraic closure and every family of LData (one for each prime), each $\text{Ш}^1(D_p,e)$ with $0<e<t$ has finite length, and for every finite set $S$ of primes

$$\sum_{l\in S}\log l\sum_{e=1}^{t-1}\operatorname{len}_{\mathbb Z_l}\text{Ш}^1(D_l,e)\le B.$$

UpperTheory.Top.IsChiField ψ e q d E says $E\subset\mathbb Q(\zeta_N)$ with $2^{e+2},\psi.N,q,d\mid N$ is the fixed field of $\{a: a\equiv\pm1 \bmod 2^{e+2},\ \psi(a)=1,\ (a/q)=(a/d)=1\}$. UpperTheory.Top.VWin asserts, for every primitive character $\psi$ (PChar), VWinAt ψ 401: for $0<\varepsilon<1/52$ and any $e(x)$ with $x^{2+8\varepsilon}\le 2^{e(x)}\le 2x^{2+8\varepsilon}$ (PageN), there are $J\ge1$ and $X_0$ such that for every WinPair $(q,d)$ at scale $X^{1/J}$ (primes $q\equiv3\bmod 8$, $d\equiv7\bmod16$, $\log q\asymp Y^{\varepsilon/5}$, $\log d\asymp Y^{\varepsilon/5}\log^2Y$, $q\mid d+1$ and every odd prime $\le Y^{\varepsilon/5}$ dividing $d+1$) one finds $Y\in[X^{1/J},X^{1/2}]$ with VVle E ⌊x^401⌋ (√exp(x^ε)) for all $x\in[Y,Y^2]$ and all such $E$. Each such $E$ is CM (UpperTheory.V.isCMField_of_isChiField, invoked in UpperTheory.V.vwinAt_of_joint).

(interpretation) By Poitou–Tate (Formalism.GaloisCohomology.PTShaAt, VFormula) these lengths are tied to $H^2(G_{E,S},\mathbb Z_l(e+1))$, so the statement is a uniform logarithmic-height bound on étale cohomology groups of the kind that control $K$-groups and zeta values at negative integers of the fields in the family.

Proof of Row0b: statement

UpperTheory.GalStar.Row0b Rc says: for every CM field $E$, prime $l$, $\iota:\overline{\mathbb Q}_l\cong\mathbb C$, $a\ge1$, cuspidal $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb A_E)$ which is regular algebraic (IsRegAlg) and polarized (GlobalTheory.ContraPair.PolarizedPair π χ), there exist $r:G_E\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb Q}_l)$, $wt$, and Hodge–Tate data $HT$ with PropsB: $r$ continuous and semisimple; Hodge–Tate multisets equal $\{(a-1)/2-z\}$ for the infinitesimal character and satisfy $HT(\tau c)=wt-HT(\tau)$; away; and above.

AwayB x y (for $x$ the Weil–Deligne representation of $r$ at $w\nmid l$ and $y=\mathrm{rc}(\pi_w)$ for every rc with Rc) contains three clauses: $\dim x=\dim y$; if $y$ has abelian Weil image (Tor) and $N=0$ then $x=y$; and if $E_w\cong\mathbb Q_g$ with $a=1$ or $g>16a^2$ (IsQpBig), $y$ is tame (TmW), square-free in the sense of Sqf, and $N_y=0$, then $N_x=0$ and $\operatorname{charpol}_x(g)=\operatorname{charpol}_y(g)$ for all $g$ of degree $0$, that is, on inertia. AboveB asserts crystallinity, de Rham and the Hodge–Tate jumps at $w\mid l$ when $\pi_w$ is spherical, and de Rham, potential semistability, jumps and the same AwayB relation for the $D_{\rm pst}$ Weil–Deligne representation when $\mathrm{rc}(\pi_w)$ falls into the tame or toral case with $N=0$.

Local Langlands: recT and LawsT

UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.IsRecT h rc means there is a local Artin map $a$ with LawsT a ha rc: dimension preservation, compatibility with characters, Langlands multisegment classification rc x = segSum rc l (Zelevinsky segments), irreducibility on cuspidals, compatibility with $|\det|^s$ twists, absolute value of the central character, and, only for Good $x$ (supercuspidal support consisting of characters, or over $\mathbb Q_g$ of twists of depth-zero cuspidals), twisting, central character, duality, injectivity and tameness. TypeLaw is Green's formula: if a depth-zero $\sigma$ inflates a cuspidal $\tau$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_{g^n})$ attached to a regular character $\theta$ with $\tau(y)=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{j<n}\theta(z^{g^j})$ on elliptic regular $y$ with eigenvalue $z$, then inertia acts through $\prod_j(X-\theta(z^{g^j}))$.

UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.recTExists builds rc from cuspEx and Zelevinsky extension exists_isLExt. cuspEx defines rc on cuspidals by if GoodCusp x then r₀ x else (J x.1).lpTw (e x), where r₀ is the depth-zero parametrization (goodMap, Par) and J is a fixed irreducible representation of the right dimension supplied by UpperTheory.Zero.LLC.junkEx, chosen over $\mathbb Q_g$ to differ from every twist of a tame representation. RecTUnique only asserts uniqueness on Good representations. (interpretation) Thus recT is a partial local Langlands correspondence, genuine on the depth-zero and principal-series part, with an arbitrary placeholder elsewhere; since Row0b quantifies over every rc satisfying IsRecT, its local conditions carry content only where LawsT pins rc down, which matches the IsQpBig, tame, toral and $N=0$ hypotheses in AwayB and AboveB.

Relation to the literature

Where the formal statement matches: Row0b corresponds to a weakened form of the theorems of Harris–Taylor, Shin and Chenevier–Harris on Galois representations for regular algebraic polarized cuspidal representations of $\mathrm{GL}_a$ over CM fields; its local–global clauses correspond to special cases of the compatibility of Caraiani and Barnet-Lamb–Gee–Geraghty–Taylor (unramified and principal-series components, depth-zero inertia at large degree-one places). FerreroWashington corresponds to the Ferrero–Washington theorem. KatoTwoW corresponds to consequences of Kato's Euler system. BlochKatoSelmerVanishes corresponds to the Bloch–Kato Selmer condition. The local Langlands content corresponds to Harris–Taylor and Henniart only on depth-zero and principal-series representations.

Points for a referee

  1. vwin_of_row and automorphic_window_of_inputs are assembly lemmas; OpenImageCondition contains only RowT, and every *_of_open lemma ignores it. The "literature theorems" (Ferrero–Washington, Poitou–Tate, Kato, Siegel–Weil, Bernstein–Zelevinsky, base change) are internal proofs whose statements should each be checked against the published theorems.
  2. The name galoisLocalGlobalStrong_recParameter overstates Row0b: AwayB gives full equality only for abelian $N=0$ parameters and inertia equality only in the tame, square-free, $N=0$ case at places with $E_w\cong\mathbb Q_g$, $g>16a^2$; AboveB gives de Rham and Hodge–Tate information only at spherical places or in those cases; no irreducibility is claimed.
  3. recT uses a placeholder junkEx on non-good supercuspidals; LawsT and RecTUnique constrain only Good representations.
  4. SeedWU4 matches Frobenius polynomials only on an open dense $V$; the passage to FrobEig at every unramified split place in limWU4_of deserves inspection.
  5. Geometric seeds are graded pieces (IsGr) of Hecke eigenspaces; HolomorphicLefschetzFormula is a major theorem proved in about 360 lines plus dependencies.
  6. TLiftK, BaseChangeIsobaricN and TwoUpOK carry hypotheses ($E/E^+$ unramified everywhere, $\sqrt{-d}\in E$, $[E^+:\mathbb Q]$ even, InertOK) whose removal in coreAll_of_core should be traced.
  7. The constant 401 is imposed by the consumer; the arithmetic theorems hold for any $C_t\ge1$. LemmaK is doubly exponential and enters only through ThmBab.
  8. VVle quantifies over all LData, with $S$ the places above $l$ only; independence of choices is a separate proposition (Formalism.GaloisCohomology.Indep).
